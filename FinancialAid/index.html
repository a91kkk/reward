What Is Financial Aid?

Financial aid can help eligible students manage educational expenses. Depending on the program, assistance may include grants, scholarships, work-study opportunities, or student loans.

Aid can have different rules, application dates, and award amounts. Some options are based on financial need, while others may consider academic performance, a field of study, community involvement, or other criteria.

Helpful tip: Check your school’s financial aid office and official program websites for current requirements. Be cautious of services that promise guaranteed funding or ask for unnecessary upfront fees.

Types of Financial Aid

Different forms of assistance work in different ways. These descriptions can help you begin your research.

🌱

Grants

Assistance that generally does not need to be repaid, subject to program conditions and continued eligibility requirements.

Usually not repaid

🏅

Scholarships

Awards based on criteria such as academic achievement, background, field of study, or other eligibility requirements.

Criteria vary

💼

Work-study

Eligible students may earn money through approved work opportunities. Positions and availability depend on the program.

Earn through work

📄

Student loans

Borrowed funds that generally must be repaid. Interest, repayment timing, and borrower protections vary.

Repayment required

Who May Want to Explore Financial Aid?

Students preparing for college, recent graduates, and people considering returning to higher education may find it useful to learn about available financial aid resources. Eligibility depends on the specific program.

Parents, guardians, and career advisers may also find it useful to review funding options with a student. Requirements can differ based on enrollment status, school, location, income, academic progress, and other factors.

How to Get Started

Take these steps at your own pace. Confirm details with the official provider.

  1. 01

    Identify your education goals

    Think about the course, school, qualification, and timeline you are considering.

  2. 02

    Research available aid programs

    Check school financial aid offices and relevant official government or scholarship resources.

  3. 03

    Review eligibility and requirements

    Note application deadlines, required documents, award conditions, and renewal rules.

  4. 04

    Use official program information

    Confirm details directly with the provider before sharing personal information or applying.
